# Research notes behind Roland's home OT programme

Researched 5 October 2026. These notes explain the choices in the manual. The manual is for home practice and doesn't replace advice from Roland's OT, Simóne Vorster.

**Evidence labels:** **[Trial/Review]** = randomised trial or systematic review · **[Study]** = single or small study · **[Practice]** = NHS or OT practice guidance (expert consensus).

## 1. Do home programmes work, and what makes them work?

- **They work when they are well set up.** In a randomised trial, parent-run OT home programmes improved children's function and parent satisfaction compared with no programme. The effective dose was about 17.5 sessions a month of about 16.5 minutes. [Trial/Review] [Novak, Cusick & Lannin 2009](https://researchonline.nd.edu.au/med_article/299)
- **What makes them work** [Trial/Review] ([Novak & Berry 2014](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25317927/)):
  - evidence-based activities
  - goals the family chose
  - a few activities the carer feels confident running
  - coaching and support for the carer
  - an exercise log
  - regular follow-up by the therapist
- **Real practice beats underlying "process" training.** In children with motor coordination difficulties, practising the actual task had a strong effect (d = 0.89); training underlying skills had a weak effect (d = 0.12). [Trial/Review] [Smits-Engelsman 2013](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23106530)
- **Finger games alone don't fix handwriting.** Hand-skill or sensory training without real writing or drawing practice did not improve handwriting, and programmes with fewer than about 20 sessions were ineffective. [Trial/Review] [Hoy, Egan & Feder 2011](https://doi.org/10.2182/cjot.2011.78.1.3)

**In the manual:**
- About 20 minutes, 5 days a week, for 9 weeks (about 45 sessions).
- Finger strength is a 3-minute warm-up; most of the time goes on balance, cutting, drawing and colouring.
- A small set of activities with photo cards, a short daily log and a check-in every second Friday.
- Reviews with the OT at week 5 and week 9.

## 2. How children learn motor skills

- **Feedback:** children learned better with feedback on every try than with reduced feedback, unlike adults. [Trial/Review] [Sullivan et al. 2008](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18339797/) The manual gives feedback on most tries for a new skill, then reduces it slowly.
- **Cue the result, not the body part.** For example "make the scissors walk on the road" rather than "move your thumb". [Study] [Wulf et al. 2010](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2010.00190/text)
- **Vary the practice once a skill is coming:** change the paper, tools and surfaces. Children who practised throws at varied distances did better than those who practised at one distance. [Study]
- **Pitch it right.** Aim for success about 4 times in 5. Missing 3 or more out of 10: make it easier. Spot-on 2 sessions in a row: make it harder. Start with errors kept low (thick lines, short distances). [Study] [Capio et al.](https://repository.hku.hk/handle/10722/198293)
- **Choice and praise.**
  - Offering a choice increases motivation, more so in children. [Trial/Review] [Patall 2008](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18298272/)
  - Specific praise for effort ("you turned the paper with your helper hand") keeps children trying after mistakes better than praising them as a person ("you're so clever"). [Study] (Cimpian 2007)

## 3. Pencil grip

- **Normal development:** a still three- or four-finger grasp at 4–6, maturing to a moving ("dynamic") tripod by about 6–7. [Practice] [ABUHB NHS](https://abuhb.nhs.wales/files/childrens-ot/school-based-occupation/developing-a-tripod-grasp-advice-pdf/)
- **Four mature grasps work equally well.** Dynamic tripod, lateral tripod, dynamic quadrupod and lateral quadrupod made no difference to handwriting speed or legibility. [Study] [Schwellnus 2012](https://experts.mcmaster.ca/scholarly-works/911178)
- **The thumb-wrap is worth changing.** Grasps with the thumb pulled in produced higher grip and pressure forces. [Study] [Schwellnus 2013](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3722657)
- **Grip aids:** use one only on the OT's advice. A small South African study found a grip helped some Grade 2 children. [Study] [Smit 2013, Wits](https://wiredspace.wits.ac.za/handle/10539/13705)
- **Practical tricks** [Practice] (NHS Fife, York NHS):
  - short or broken crayons
  - golf and triangular pencils
  - a pompom under the ring and little fingers
  - "pinch and flip" to pick the pencil up
  - upright surfaces
  - setting the grip at the start instead of nagging mid-task

## 4. Left-handed set-up

- **Paper:**
  - to the left of his midline
  - tilted **clockwise** 30–45°, with the left corner higher
  - the writing hand stays below the line, which prevents a hooked wrist
  - pencil held 2–3 cm from the tip

  Sources: [Teach Handwriting](https://www.teachhandwriting.co.uk/paper-position-for-comfortable-handwriting.html), [West Suffolk NHS](https://www.wsh.nhs.uk/CMS-Documents/Services/Integrated-Community-Paediatric-Services-OT/g.-Handwriting.pdf), [Sussex Community NHS](https://www.sussexcommunity.nhs.uk/patients-and-visitors/resources/patient-resources/childrens-occupational-therapy-resource-for-primary-schools-0/handwriting).
- **The OT's wording needs checking.** The report says "rotate the page 45 degrees to the left". In standard wording a tilt "to the left" (anticlockwise) is the right-hander's position. She most likely meant "on his left with the left corner raised". **Confirm with Simóne.**
- **Scissors:**
  - Use true left-handed scissors, with the blades reversed so he can see the line.
  - "Ambidextrous" scissors often have right-handed blades.
  - Left-handers cut around shapes clockwise.

  Sources: [ABUHB NHS](https://abuhb.nhs.wales/files/childrens-ot/school-based-occupation/school-based-equipment/first-left-handed-scissors-pdf/), [Cambs NHS](https://cambspborochildrenshealth.nhs.uk/child-development-and-growing-up/hand-skills/scissor-skills/).
- **Seating:** he sits at the left end of a shared table. Light comes from his right.

## 6. Primitive reflex programmes

The evidence is weak: few, small studies, and no convincing benefit for learning. [Trial/Review] ([review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9301367/))

The manual includes crawling, cat-cow, egg and superman as general motor play, because they also build strength and body awareness. Do only the reflex exercises the OT prescribes, and avoid paid commercial reflex programmes.

## 7. Sitting and fatigue

- **Sitting:**
  - feet flat with hips, knees and ankles at right angles
  - back against the chair
  - table about 5 cm above the bent elbow
  - a slant board helps

  [Practice] ([NHS Cambridgeshire](https://cambspborochildrenshealth.nhs.uk/services/cambridgeshire-childrens-occupational-therapy-service/sitting-position-and-posture/), West Suffolk NHS)
- **Signs he is tired:**
  - switching hands
  - propping his head
  - slumping
  - pressing much harder or lighter
  - tighter grip
  - messier work
  - silliness or tears

  Roland already shows the first two, so the log records them.
- **Why children switch hands:** fatigue, or avoiding crossing the midline. If he switches, hand the crayon back once ("left hand draws, right hand helps"). If he switches again within a minute, stop and move.

## Caveats

- Most home-programme trials are in children with cerebral palsy or autism, so applying them to Roland is a reasonable extrapolation, not a direct match.
- Some sources were read as abstracts or summaries because full texts were not reachable from the research environment.
- Shop prices in the shopping list are estimates from 5 October 2026.
